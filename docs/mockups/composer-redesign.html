A · today Two stacked frames

The deck bar is its own bordered row with a background, and the composer field is a second framed box below it. Two competing containers — the controls read as belonging to the transcript above, even though they govern the message you're about to send.

What hurts

  • Two borders, two backgrounds stacked — the eye has to parse which box owns what
  • Plan mode is invisible: buried three clicks deep inside the "Full access" menu
  • Stats (cost / tokens / duration) sit at equal weight with the send controls
  • The wide ↑ Send pill costs horizontal room the deck bar needs
  • Branch/worktree — Orchestra's whole premise — is nowhere near the send action
B · recommended One card — controls docked inside the field

The deck bar dissolves into the bottom row of the composer card. One frame, one focus ring. Send becomes a circular icon (the label moves to the tooltip), which buys back the width for the menus. A quiet strip beneath the card carries the ambient context — host, worktree, context gauge, branch — at caption weight so it recedes until you look for it.

C · B + mode toggle Plan / Build promoted to a first-class segmented control

Everything in B, plus the highest-frequency decision — am I planning or building? — comes out of the permission dropdown and onto the bar as a two-up segment. This is the one T3 affordance that changes behaviour rather than looks: right now plan mode is discoverable only if you already know it exists.

⚠
The one real design question in C. Plan mode is currently a value of sdkPermissionMode (plan sits alongside acceptEdits, bypassPermissions…). Promoting it to its own toggle means two controls write the same field — picking "Plan" has to visibly change the permission chip, or they'll drift and contradict each other on screen. Cleanest fix: the permission chip hides the plan entry and shows Plan as a read-only state while the segment is on Plan.

not taking Three things from T3 I'd deliberately leave

Rejected

  • Their tall default input. T3's box is ~4 empty rows. You're scanning transcripts across 40+ agents — that's 100px of nothing pushing content off screen. Our rows={1} auto-grow is better.
  • Their terse placeholder ("Ask for follow-up changes"). Ours teaches /, ! and paste. That discoverability is worth more than the brevity.
  • Their High · Normal · 1M triple-chip. Three orthogonal axes crammed into one label. Our EffortSlider panel is clearer; I'd only shorten the trigger to High · 1M.
